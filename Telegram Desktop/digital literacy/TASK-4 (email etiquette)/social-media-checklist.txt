Social Media Do's:
				
1. Do keep your profiles professional. Use a clear, appropriate profile photo and write a bio that reflects your academic identity — especially on LinkedIn and GitHub. 

2. Do think before you post. Ask yourself: "Would I be comfortable if my professor, parent, or future employer saw this?" If not, do not post it. 


3. Do use privacy settings. Review and set privacy controls on Instagram, Facebook, and Twitter/X so that personal posts are visible only to trusted connections.
 
4. Do give credit when sharing others' work. If you share someone's article, photo, or idea, tag them or mention the source. Plagiarism applies online too. 


5. Do engage positively and constructively. Comment with useful insights, ask genuine questions, and support peers' achievements — this builds your online reputation.



Social media don’ts:


1. Don't share your OTPs, passwords, or PINs.
No legitimate organisation will ask for these via social media DMs or comments.
 
2.  Don't post or share unverified news. Check sources before forwarding anything on WhatsApp or Twitter/X. Misinformation spreads fast and causes real harm. 

3. Don't engage with online trolls or abusive users. Block, mute, and report — responding only escalates the situation and harms your own wellbeing.

4. Don't overshare location data. Avoid tagging your live location, checking in at home, or posting real-time updates about where you are alone.

5. Don't use social media as a substitute for professional communication. Sending informal messages or complaints about professors/institutions on public platforms can have serious academic and professional consequences.
